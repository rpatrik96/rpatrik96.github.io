Toy simulation, seeded and deterministic: each synthetic “text” has binary honesty and formality attributes, the fixed feature computes z₁₇ = 1.7 · formality + Gaussian noise, and alignment is the held-out accuracy of sign(z₁₇) against the shipped annotation. Built for this tutorial as an illustration of the paper’s “Alignment ≠ Ontological Identity” inferential gap; it is not an experiment from the paper, and the sample sentences are invented.

What this shows

The feature z₁₇ never changes: it is one scalar activation, computed the same way for every text (in this toy it responds to register, i.e. formal vs. informal style). What changes across the three panels is only the contrast set π — which texts the analyst evaluates on, and which annotation ships with them. On π_A every honest text is formal and every dishonest text informal, so the honesty and formality annotations induce the same split; sign(z₁₇) matches either one on the same ~96% of held-out texts, and an analyst holding honesty labels publishes “z₁₇ represents honesty”. On π_B the identical feature earns the label “formality”. Between the first two rows of the ledger, nothing about z changed — only π did.

The crossed set π_C is the resolution the paper’s mismatch box calls for: supervision that breaks the co-variation. Once dishonest-formal and honest-informal texts exist, the two labellings come apart — alignment with formality survives while alignment with honesty falls to roughly chance. Saying “z₁₇ represents honesty” after π_A alone therefore admits a stronger, contrast-invariant reading than the evidence licenses; the defensible claim is indexed by the contrast set, i.e. z₁₇ aligns with honesty under π_A. The fourth mode, π(q), turns the same lesson into a dial: the smaller the fraction of crossed texts, the longer the wrong label survives the toy’s ≥75% publishing bar.

What the paper says

“The label is a property of the pair (z, π) and not an intrinsic property of z. When concepts co-vary under π, the data are consistent with multiple labellings, e.g. honesty and formality may be indistinguishable if they correlate on the contrast set (Chang et al., 2009).”

— arXiv:2602.16698, “Alignment ≠ Ontological Identity” mismatch box (Gap)

“Alignment is contrast-dependent, so the tuple (z, π, c) is more representative than (z, c).”

— arXiv:2602.16698, “Alignment ≠ Ontological Identity” mismatch box (Takeaway)

In the tuple, z is the feature (here z₁₇), π the contrast set — the texts the analyst evaluates on — and c the concept whose annotation the feature is scored against (here honesty or formality). The annotation belongs to the concept c, not to π; that is why the same π_A texts can support two labels.

Check its “What would resolve it” answer: multiple contrast sets plus supervision that breaks co-variation.
  3. Pick a feature-labelling result you know (an SAE feature name, a probing claim) and write down its implicit contrast set π. Then design the second contrast set you would collect to test whether the label survives. Compare with a neighbour: did you break the same confound?

What this shows

Every probe on the capacity slider — from the 13-parameter linear probe to the 673-parameter MLP — reads the same frozen 12-dimensional representation; only the probe changes. Every capacity reaches high accuracy on the real labels, so that number alone cannot distinguish “the representation encodes the concept” from “the probe is expressive enough to fit any labelling”. The shuffled-label control makes the second explanation visible: as capacity grows, the control curve climbs toward the real one and the selectivity gap shrinks continuously to nothing. The y-axis is training-set accuracy, which is exactly where probe memorisation shows up. Hewitt and Liang’s actual protocol is stricter than the toy’s — control labels assigned consistently per word type, probes scored on held-out data — whereas the toy shuffles labels per example and reports training accuracy: a deliberately simplified analogue built to surface the same mechanism.

The toy mirrors the paper’s worked example. A linear probe on BERT’s hidden states reaches over 97% accuracy at predicting part-of-speech tags (Hewitt and Manning, 2019), yet Hewitt and Liang (2019) showed that probes of similar capacity reach high accuracy on random control labels that carry no linguistic content. Extractability is not use, either: Elazar et al. (2021) removed the information exploited by POS probes via causal intervention and found minimal effect on BERT’s language modelling. High probe accuracy admits a stronger reading than the evidence supports; reporting selectivity alongside accuracy separates the two readings.

What the paper says

“Hewitt and Liang (2019) introduced ‘control tasks’—random label assignments that probes can nonetheless fit—demonstrating that high probe accuracy does not entail that representations encode the probed property; the probe may simply memorise.”

“The ‘selectivity’ of a probe—accuracy on the real task minus accuracy on controls—is a better estimand than raw accuracy.”

— Causality is Key for Interpretability Claims to Generalise (arXiv:2602.16698), on probing evidence

estimand — the precise quantity an analysis sets out to estimate. Selectivity is a sharper estimand than raw accuracy, but still an associational one (rung 1 of the causal ladder: patterns in observed data, no intervention on the model); whether the model uses the concept is a separate, causal estimand.

Try this

  1. Stay on the linear probe and read only the purple number. Write the one-sentence claim you would put in an abstract. Now drag the slider to 48 hidden units: does your sentence survive, and which word in it had to change? Sweep back down and find the width where you would stop believing the claim.
  2. The tutorial’s live case: a probe that “detects gender” in a model’s activations scores just as high when the labels are randomly shuffled. Which reported number would have caught this before publication: accuracy, or selectivity?
  3. Even the linear probe’s +32.5-point selectivity only supports a decodability claim. What experiment would you run to argue the model uses the concept? (Interventions come later in the tutorial.)

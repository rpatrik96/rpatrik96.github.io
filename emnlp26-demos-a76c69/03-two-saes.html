What this shows

Both runs minimise the same objective on the same 500 activations: reconstruction error plus an ℓ1 sparsity penalty. Their objective values land within a few percent of each other, yet the best one-to-one match between their decoder dictionaries stays well below |cos| = 1: some features correspond almost exactly, others split, merge, or have no counterpart in the other run. Each live feature still looks interpretable in isolation — its top-activating contexts cluster around coherent word categories. Interpretable-looking features plus a low loss are exactly the evidence an SAE analysis typically reports, and here two different dictionaries supply that evidence equally well. This is the situation in the paper’s Case Study II (proxy gaming): reconstruction and sparsity are proxies, and, as the paper notes, it is known that proxies such as reconstruction error or the ℓ0 norm cannot be used for model selection (Locatello et al., 2019). Auto-interpretability scores do not break the tie either: the paper cites evidence that they can attain high values even on random structure (Heap et al., 2025).

What would break the tie is identifiability: conditions on the data under which the recovered representation is unique up to permutation and scaling — that is, every acceptable solution is the same dictionary reordered and rescaled, one equivalence class of answers. (The overlap meter’s “permutation & sign” is the same class: decoder directions are unit-norm, so rescaling collapses to a sign flip.) The paper points to sufficient-variability conditions (Lachapelle et al., 2022) — the concepts must vary enough across contexts rather than always co-occurring — and to sparse shift autoencoders trained on paired samples (Joshi et al., 2025), i.e. pairs of contexts that differ in only a few concepts, as concrete routes. At the default correlation setting the toy sits in the opposite regime — co-occurring, co-varying concepts in superposition, no paired data — so many sparse factorisations tie on the objective in principle, the two runs surface just two of these near-optima, and the training seed decides which dictionary you would have reported. The slider makes the diagnosis playable: drop the correlation toward 0 and both runs recover nearly all six true concept directions; raise it and recovery falls as features blur into seed-dependent mixtures, while the objective values stay close throughout. Identification tracks the data, not the loss value.

What the paper says

“Multiple sparse factorisations can achieve similar objective values. Thus, while sparsity is a useful inductive bias, it does not guarantee identification.”

— Case Study II: Proxy Gaming, arXiv:2602.16698

“However, even if SAE features are interpretable, that implies neither uniqueness nor identifiability.”

— § “The Right Question Is Not Enough. Answers Must Be Identifiable”, arXiv:2602.16698

Try this

  1. Retrain Run B several times. The objective stays within a few percent of Run A while the dictionary reshuffles. What extra evidence would you need before calling either run’s features “the model’s features”?
  2. Select the cleanest-looking feature in Run A (one dominant concept bar), then check its closest match in Run B. Now do the same for a mixed feature. Which kind survives the seed change, and why might correlated concepts matter? Then drop the concept-correlation slider to 0 and watch the truth check: both runs now recover nearly all six concept directions.
  3. Both runs score similarly on reconstruction and sparsity, and both look interpretable feature-by-feature. Name one data property, experiment, or assumption that would rule one dictionary out — then compare with the “What would resolve it” step of Case Study II: verify that the data varies enough for the answer to be unique (or collect paired contexts), rather than pointing to a low loss.

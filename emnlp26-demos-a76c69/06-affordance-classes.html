Affordances Shrink the Equivalence Class

Two latent concepts in an LLM’s residual stream — say, sentiment and tense. Every dot is a rival explanation that fits your evidence; each interaction you can afford — paired samples, environments, interventions, or an assumption — rules out a different family of rivals, and identifiability names what survives.

What this shows

The toy has two latent concepts (read them as sentiment and tense), and each dot is a candidate explanation — a transformation of the hoped-for coordinates that reproduces every piece of evidence gathered so far. With observational samples alone, all of them fit: the cloud is the equivalence class, and the starred “true” coordinates are indistinguishable from any other member. Each affordance you switch on is an interaction that makes some rivals visibly wrong: paired concept shifts rule out cross-mixing and nonlinear warps (the paper’s SSAE example, where latents are “identified up to permutations and scaling”), multi-environment contrasts rule out cross-mixing once enough environments supply variability (slide the environments control and watch the band shrink), and single-concept interventions additionally pin which coordinate is which. Combinations intersect — in the paper’s words, “the resulting equivalence class reflects the joint constraints imposed by all available affordances.”

Two things are worth noticing at the controls. First, different affordance sets can induce the same class — sparsity, an assumption, buys the same shrinkage here as multi-environment data with plenty of environments, which is exactly where the free lunch is repaid. Second, the class never collapses to a point: even with everything on, per-concept rescalings (drawn as dot size) survive, so an honest claim is always “identified up to ∼”. This is the paper’s affordance lens in one picture: “What an interpreter can discover about a model depends on the interactions available to them” (the “Concepts as Affordances” box, citing Gibson, 1979) — so an interpretability claim should state which interactions back it, and which rivals those interactions were ever able to rule out.

What the paper says

“The key insight is threefold: (i) affordances define which variations are observable or inducible; (ii) these variations break equivalence classes among candidate representations such that two representations that behave identically under all afforded interactions are, for our purposes, equivalent; (iii) identifiability characterises precisely which structure survives this winnowing.” — Appendix, “The Metaphysics of Affordance-based Identifiability”
“There is no free lunch in interpretability: supervision enters both through the affordances a method is designed to leverage (rather than labelled datasets), and through downstream interpretation; unsupervised objectives and evaluations do not, by themselves, distinguish representations that uncover meaningful variables from those that do not.” — “Relocating supervision”, in “The Right Question Is Not Enough. Answers Must Be Identifiable”

Try this

  1. Find two different affordance sets that draw the same dashed outline. What does that imply for “method A vs. method B” debates when both methods leverage the same interactions — and what would you need to change to tell them apart?
  2. Switch everything on. The class still contains more than one dot. Say precisely what a claim of “we found the true features” would have to be weakened to — and why “identified up to permutation and rescaling” is a success, not a failure.
  3. Pick a method you use — probing, SAEs, activation patching — and write down its affordance set: what does it observe, what does it pair, what does it intervene on? Which dots in this cloud would it never be able to rule out?

Pointwise · the evidence (L1)
∇f(x)│x₀  or  f(x₀) − f(x₀ with token i → b)
a property of (model, input, analyst choice)
Distributional · the claim’s estimand (L2)
𝔼x∼p[f(x) − f(x∖i)]
fixed once the intervention family and reference distribution p are specified

L1/L2 = rungs one and two of Pearl’s ladder of causation (see the Causal Ladder demo): L1 is what the observed evidence at hand shows, L2 is what an explicit intervention establishes.

What this shows

All six analyst choices read the same frozen network at the same input, and the prediction never moves from f(x₀) = +3.00. Occlusion with a zero baseline says “bad” matters most; swap in the mean embedding and the comma takes over; a random baseline hands the top spot to “not” at ten times the mean-baseline scale; plain gradient saliency ranks “bad” — the token the toy model was built around — last, and an invertible change of input coordinates reshuffles the gradient ranking again. The blend slider makes the arbitrariness continuous: sweeping between two defensible-looking baselines, the crown changes hands at flip points no analyst would defend while the printed magnitudes inflate roughly ten-fold. None of these knobs touches a model parameter. Each panel answers a question about (model, input, choice); quoting one as “token i is what the model relies on” elevates local sensitivity into a distributional claim the evidence does not license.

The fix is not a better baseline but a better-specified estimand. The aggregated row computes the expected effect of an explicit deletion intervention across a reference distribution of jittered inputs and across all four baseline choices, with the spread reported as a standard error. Under that estimand the ranking is stable: “not” and “bad”, the interaction the toy was built around, separate cleanly from every other token, and the row stays put no matter which pointwise knob you flip, because the knobs are marginalised rather than silently fixed. The same failure modes are documented for real models — saliency maps remain stable under parameter randomization (Adebayo et al., 2018), attributions shift under semantics-preserving transformations (Kindermans et al., 2019), and SHAP values distribute arbitrarily among correlated features (Merrick and Taly, 2020).

What the paper says

“The quantity depends on arbitrary choices—baseline, parameterisation, perturbation scheme—that are not properties of the model; different choices yield different attributions (Adebayo et al., 2018; Sundararajan et al., 2017; Ancona et al., 2018). Even if local sensitivity were uniquely defined, pointwise evidence does not establish distributional claims.”

“Saliency measures local sensitivity whereas global importance requires distributional aggregation over explicit interventions.”

— Causality is Key for Interpretability Claims to Generalise (arXiv:2602.16698), mismatch box “Local Sensitivity ≠ Global Importance”

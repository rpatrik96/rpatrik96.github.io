      How this is scored

      Points. Right: sure +4, leaning +3, guess +1. Wrong: sure −6, leaning −2, guess +1. The table is built so that the point-maximising choice is the honest one: “leaning” pays more than “guess” once you believe above 60%, and “sure” pays more than “leaning” above 80%, the midpoints between the stated 50 / 70 / 90. Sure-and-wrong is the expensive case, as it is in review.

      Calibration. A forecaster who says “70%” should be right on 70% of those days. For each confidence level, your stated probability (which is lower for four-option items, where a guess is 25%) is compared with how often you were right at that level; the weighted average of the gaps is the calibration error, the same quantity as expected calibration error for a classifier. The over/under verdict uses the signed gap.

      Level. Points count toward your level only the first time you see an item, so repeating a round does not level you up. A flawless, all-sure round reaches the top level; in practice it takes more than one domain.

      The answers are opinionated: one reviewer's taste, argued in each reveal. Disagreeing with a reason is allowed. That is also how taste gets built.
